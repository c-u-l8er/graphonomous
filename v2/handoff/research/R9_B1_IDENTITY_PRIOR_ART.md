# R9 — Prior art for the G0-B.1 identity rulings (researched 2026-09-03)

Scope: the four GPT rulings of 2026-09-03 — (1) relation = proposition, citation = separate ASSERTION; (2) anonymous finding id = hash(container_lid ‖ NUL ‖ sentence); (3) bare identifier resolved-if-unique + UNQUALIFIED_REFERENCE, ambiguous → AMBIGUOUS_IDENTIFIER and no edge; (4) stratified Datalog with per-fact JTMS-shaped derivations replayed by an independent checker. Every bullet ends with its source URL; where a source could not be fetched the bullet says so.

## A. RDF 1.2 triple terms vs reifiers; Wikidata statements; nanopublications

- RDF 1.2 Concepts is a **W3C Candidate Recommendation Snapshot dated 7 April 2026**; the TR history lists WDs from 16 May 2023 through 3 April 2026 and the CR Snapshot as the latest entry — **no Proposed Recommendation exists as of 2026-09-03** (https://www.w3.org/standards/history/rdf12-concepts/).
- The CR call (7 April 2026) covers Concepts and Semantics, comments due 5 May 2026; Semantics says it is "not expected to advance to Recommendation any earlier than 05 May 2026"; no feature is marked at-risk in the announcement (https://www.w3.org/news/2026/w3c-invites-implementations-of-rdf-1-2-concepts-and-abstract-data-model-and-rdf-1-2-semantics/).
- Definitions kept in the CR: "An RDF triple used as the object of another triple is called a triple term"; "A reifying triple is a triple where the predicate is rdf:reifies and the object is a triple term. The subject of that triple is called a reifier"; "A reifier may denote a variety of things related to the triple term's proposition, such as a statement or belief that the proposition holds"; triple terms are "transparent" (same denotation as when asserted) (https://www.w3.org/TR/rdf12-concepts/).
- The proposition/occurrence split survives and is many-to-many: "There can be multiple, distinct reifiers related to the same abstract proposition" and "One reifier may also be used to reify multiple, distinct propositions"; "It is expected that the reifiers (rather than the triple terms) will be used in further statements" (https://www.w3.org/TR/rdf12-concepts/).
- RDF 1.2 Semantics (CR Snapshot, 7 April 2026): "the object of a reifying triple always denotes a proposition", axiom `rdf:reifies rdfs:range rdfs:Proposition`; nothing makes rdf:reifies functional or inverse-functional (https://www.w3.org/TR/rdf12-semantics/).
- The WG declined to define a reifier *as* an occurrence: issue #169 "definition of reifiers is non-normative and seems vague" (opened 2025-06-26 by rat10, asking that reifiers be characterised as tokens/occurrences of triple terms) was closed 2025-09-25, state_reason "completed", 45 comments, closing note "At the WG meeting today it was decided to close this issue" — the "may denote a variety of things" wording stands (https://api.github.com/repos/w3c/rdf-star-wg/issues/169).
- Syntax follows the model: RDF 1.2 Turtle WD (12 Aug 2026) — "While a reifiedTriple may contain at most one reifier, the annotation syntax may contain any number of reifiers. Each reifier causes a corresponding reifying triple to be produced"; an omitted reifier gets "a fresh RDF blank node" (https://www.w3.org/TR/2026/WD-rdf12-turtle-20260812/). Primer DNOTE (26 Aug 2026): the triple term "denotes the proposition itself, as a logical, abstract object identified through its constituent subject, predicate, and object components"; "We can also annotate statements with multiple reifiers" (https://www.w3.org/TR/2026/DNOTE-rdf12-primer-20260826/).
- Wikidata practice: a statement "consists of a claim (a property-value pair … together with optional qualifiers), augmented by references (giving the source for the claim) and a rank" (https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Wikidata:Glossary); the statement `id` is "An arbitrary identifier for the Statement, which is unique across the repository", and "References record provenance information … They are given as a list of reference records" each with its own `hash`, `snaks`, `snaks-order` (https://doc.wikimedia.org/Wikibase/master/php/docs_topics_json.html); editors are told to add a reference to the existing statement rather than duplicate the claim — "It is recommended to not add a second reference which is explicitly based on the same source" (https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Help:Sources).
- Nanopublications: "implemented with the help of named RDF graphs, with one graph for each of assertion, provenance, and publication info, plus an additional head graph"; "identified by URIs that contain a hash value that is calculated on their entire content" — i.e. the nanopub id covers assertion **and** its provenance together (an assertion-level id, not a proposition-level one) (https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1809.06532). The provenance graph points at the assertion graph URI (`:assertion prov:wasAttributedTo …`, `prov:wasDerivedFrom …`) (https://nanopub.readthedocs.io/en/latest/publishing/setting-subgraphs.html).

## B. Skolemization and deterministic blank-node identity

- RDF 1.1 Concepts (Recommendation, 25 Feb 2014) §3.4: "Blank node identifiers are local identifiers … always locally scoped to the file or RDF store, and are not persistent or portable identifiers for blank nodes"; "Blank nodes do not have identifiers in the RDF abstract syntax" (https://www.w3.org/TR/rdf11-concepts/#section-blank-nodes).
- §3.5 Skolemization: systems "SHOULD mint a new, globally unique IRI (a Skolem IRI) for each blank node so replaced"; recognisable ones use a path starting `/.well-known/genid/`, which "allows a system to map IRIs back to blank nodes if needed". The spec requires only global uniqueness — it says nothing about deriving the IRI from content or context (https://www.w3.org/TR/rdf11-concepts/).
- RDFC-1.0 (Recommendation, 21 May 2024) derives labels from the neighbourhood: Hash First Degree Quads takes the quads that *mention* the blank node, replaces it by `_:a` and other blank nodes by `_:z`, serialises to canonical N-Quads, sorts, concatenates, SHA-256s; ties escalate to Hash N-Degree Quads over "gossip paths" through connected blank nodes; "To determine a canonical labeling, RDFC-1.0 considers the information connected to each blank node". Labels `c14n0, c14n1…` are assigned in code-point order of the hashes; each quad is "terminated by a single line feed character (U+000A)" before hashing. The label is therefore a function of the surrounding graph, not of the node (https://www.w3.org/TR/rdf-canon/).
- RDFC-1.0 also warns it is a "partial canonicalization function" — pathological datasets can prevent termination and implementations should abort early; the fixed-depth hash in ruling (2) avoids this by construction (https://www.w3.org/TR/rdf-canon/).
- Hogan 2017 (ACM TWEB 11(4), 22:1–62) computes iso-canonical forms by adapting Nauty and lists "applying consistent Skolemisation schemes where blank nodes are mapped to IRIs" as a direct application — deterministic skolem IRIs from graph-context labels are established practice (https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3068333).
- Trusty URIs (module RA): sort quads, replace the artifact's own URI by a blank, serialise "with a newline character at the end of each", SHA-256 + base64; "Blank nodes are not supported and have to be skolemized in the same way when a trusty URI is produced" — content-addressed identity forces deterministic skolemization upstream, exactly the pressure ruling (2) answers (https://github.com/trustyuri/trustyuri-spec).

## C. Reference-resolution discipline (CURIE, JSON-LD, identifiers.org, Bioregistry)

- W3C CURIE Syntax 1.0 (WG Note, 16 Dec 2010): `[[prefix] ':'] reference`; "There MUST be a prefix binding for the prefix (or the default prefix, if the prefix is absent) in scope"; without a default prefix "CURIEs MUST NOT be used without a leading prefix and colon"; safe CURIEs `[…]` exist purely to disambiguate from IRIs — the spec forbids rather than resolves bare references (https://www.w3.org/TR/curie/).
- JSON-LD 1.1 API (Recommendation, 16 July 2020) §5.2 IRI Expansion: term definitions are checked before compact IRIs; "If value contains a colon, split the value into a prefix and suffix at the first colon"; "If prefix is underscore (_) or suffix begins with double-forward-slash (//), return value as it is already an absolute IRI or a blank node identifier"; expansion happens only if the prefix is a defined term with an IRI mapping (and, in 1.1, the `prefix` flag). An unknown prefix is left untouched — silently, with no error (https://www.w3.org/TR/json-ld11-api/).
- JSON-LD 1.1 Syntax defines the compact IRI as `prefix:suffix` and gives term definitions precedence over compact-IRI interpretation — the "term vs compact IRI" rule is *precedence*, not uniqueness search (https://www.w3.org/TR/json-ld11/).
- identifiers.org (Wimalaratne et al., Sci Data 5:180029, 2018): compact identifier = "a namespace prefix, a separating colon, and a locally unique identifier (LUI), e.g. pdb:2gc4", optionally `provider/prefix:LUI`; embedded prefixes "are ignored where the result of concatenation would be duplication of the prefix and the colon, so a compact identifier example is e.g. GO:0003214, not GO:GO:0003214"; behaviour for an unknown prefix is not specified in the paper (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5944906/). The current scheme page restates prefix ':' accession with optional provider code (https://docs.identifiers.org/pages/identification_scheme).
- Bioregistry (Hoyt et al., Sci Data 9, 2022; bioRxiv v3 text): the same LUI "can be represented by at least seven distinct URIs and three distinct CURIEs"; "NCBITaxon appears in 17 external registries under 10 different prefixes"; capitalisation heterogeneity "go vs. GO"; redundant prefixes "GO:GO:0006915" — prefix ambiguity is the norm, so unprefixed local ids are unresolvable without a namespace (https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2022.07.08.499378v3.full).
- Bioregistry "banana": "a redundant prefix and delimiter that appears in local unique identifiers, making them look like a CURIE" (e.g. `ocid:ocid:190000021540`); "The Bioregistry will automatically strip off the banana when standardizing" — normalisation is silent, no fault is surfaced (https://bioregistry.io/ocid). The `curies` library standardises via curated `prefix_synonyms` (`standardize_prefix("gomf") → "GO"`) and resolves overlapping URI prefixes by longest match; the tutorial does not state what an unknown prefix returns (https://curies.readthedocs.io/en/latest/tutorial.html).
- Net: no standard above resolves a namespace-less token by searching for a unique match; they either require a binding (CURIE), leave the string as-is (JSON-LD), or require a prefix and canonicalise its *form* (identifiers.org, Bioregistry). Ruling (3)'s resolve-when-unique is more permissive than all of them; its fault is the compensating novelty; "ambiguous → no edge" matches CURIE's MUST (https://www.w3.org/TR/curie/).

## D. Datalog provenance, proof trees, independent checkers, JTMS

- Green–Karvounarakis–Tannen, PODS 2007: "relational algebra calculations for incomplete databases, probabilistic databases, bag semantics and why-provenance are particular cases of the same general algorithms involving semirings … a comprehensive provenance representation that uses semirings of polynomials. We extend these considerations to datalog and semirings of formal power series" (abstract extracted from the PDF; the ACM DL page returned 403) (https://web.cs.ucdavis.edu/~green/papers/pods07.pdf).
- Soufflé provenance: run with `-t explain` (stdout CLI) or `-t explore` (ncurses); `explain path(1, 3)` prints a proof tree with rule labels R1, R2…; each tuple carries the producing rule number and "the height of a minimal height proof tree"; `format json|proof` and `output <file>` export the tree. The page describes no checker separate from the evaluator (https://souffle-lang.github.io/provenance).
- Nemo (Ivliev et al., KR 2024): "Nemo also supports tracing, i.e., it can provide a justification for its inferences by producing a proof tree that shows the concrete rule applications and input facts used to obtain a conclusion … tracing is very efficient and adds no cost to reasoning"; "Nemo, RDFox, and Soufflé all have tracing capabilities that return single proof trees" (https://proceedings.kr.org/2024/70/kr2024-0070-ivliev-et-al.pdf). CLI: `nmo rules.rls --trace "p(1,3);q(3)"` prints an ASCII tree (https://knowsys.github.io/nemo-doc/installation/cli/); releases add `--trace-input-file`, "Support Trace as DAG in GraphML format" (v0.6.0, 4 Oct 2022) and `--trace-all-idb-facts` (v0.10.1, 1 Aug 2024) (https://github.com/knowsys/nemo/releases).
- Independent checker: Tantow, Gerlach, Mennicke, Krötzsch, "Verifying Datalog Reasoning with Lean", ITP 2025 (LIPIcs 352, 36:1–36:19, published 22 Sep 2025) — "a certificate-based approach to validate results of Datalog reasoners in a formally verified checker for Datalog proofs" in Lean, verified "against direct formalizations of the Datalog semantics" (least-model and proof-theoretic, proved equivalent); two JSON encodings, "Datalog proof trees" and "directed acyclic graphs for succinctness"; certificates come "by converting derivation traces of an existing Datalog reasoner" (https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ITP.2025.36). Code: "a checker for Datalog proof trees and proofs encoded as directed acyclic graphs … formally verified in Lean 4", binary `.lake/build/bin/certifyingDatalog` (https://github.com/knowsys/CertifyingDatalog).
- Coq lineage: "Certified Graph View Maintenance with Regular Datalog" (2018) proves an *evaluator* "sound with respect to the declarative RD semantics" — a verified evaluator, not a certificate checker (https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.10565).
- Replay as checking: "verifying a provenance witness for a query involves re-executing the Datalog program on the witness, which is exactly re-executing a firing sequence" (2026) — the witness is validated by replay, which is ruling (4)'s checker shape (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.26725).
- JTMS shape: Doyle 1979, *Artificial Intelligence* 12(3):231–272 — a TMS "recording and maintaining the reasons for program beliefs"; nodes carry support-list (SL: IN-list/OUT-list) and conditional-proof (CP) justifications; "Each statement having at least one valid justification is made a part of the current belief set" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reason_maintenance). The original PDF exceeded the fetch size limit and the PhilPapers/Semantic Scholar abstract pages returned 403/empty, so the quote above is Wikipedia's paraphrase; the journal PDF is at (https://cse.buffalo.edu/~rapaport/Papers/Papers.by.Others/NONMONOTONIC/doyle79.pdf).
- Which ship an evaluator-independent checker: only Tantow et al./CertifyingDatalog. Soufflé and Nemo emit per-fact proof trees (rule id + antecedents, DAG-shareable in Nemo) but the tree is the evaluator's own report; GKT is a representation, not a tool (https://github.com/knowsys/CertifyingDatalog).

## E. 2025–2026 material on "citation must not contaminate relation identity"

- **For.** RDF 1.2 Concepts CR (7 Apr 2026) is the strongest normative statement: the triple term *is* the proposition; each source gets its own reifier; "multiple, distinct reifiers related to the same abstract proposition, such as statements with different sources"; provenance hangs off the reifier, never the triple term (https://www.w3.org/TR/rdf12-concepts/).
- **For, with a caveat.** Issue #169 (2025-06-26 → closed 2025-09-25) shows the WG refusing to *fix* the reifier as "an occurrence"; the identity split is kept but the reifier's meaning is left open ("statement or belief … event or circumstance"). A profile that says "our reifier = a citation occurrence" is stricter than RDF 1.2, not contrary to it (https://github.com/w3c/rdf-star-wg/issues/169).
- **For.** RDF 1.2 Schema WD (28 Mar 2026) §7.2 on old-style reification: "The subject of a reification is intended to denote a concrete realization of an RDF triple, such as a document in a surface syntax, rather than a triple considered as an abstract object … provenance information … meaningful only when thought of as denoting a particular instance or token of a triple" (https://www.w3.org/TR/2026/WD-rdf12-schema-20260328/).
- **For (practitioner).** Lassila & Gschwend, 11 May 2026: provenance, confidence and source attach to the reifier; "multiple sources assert identical triples" is named as the enterprise driver (https://knowledgegraphinsights.com/rdf12/). Cagle, 22 Mar 2026: "The reifier ex:assertion1 *is* that triple occurrence", contrasted with property graphs where every edge instance has its own id even for identical endpoints/type — the opposite discipline (https://ontologist.substack.com/p/rdf-12-vs-neo4jopencypher). Cagle & Shannon, 24 Apr 2026: the `~` sigil "assigns a named IRI to the reification itself" as a first-class, promotable resource (https://ontologist.substack.com/p/the-event-made-visible).
- **For (practice).** Wikidata keeps one statement per claim with a list of references, each reference content-hashed separately; guidance forbids a second statement just to add a source (page revised 16 Jul 2026) (https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Help:Sources).
- **For.** PROV-O (Rec, 30 Apr 2013): "The Qualification Pattern restates an unqualified influence relation by using an intermediate class that represents the influence between two resources" — the binary relation is kept; attribution/time live on the qualifier node (https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-o/).
- **Against (by design).** SPDX 3.0.1: `Relationship` is itself an Element with mandatory `spdxId` and `creationInfo` plus `from`/`to`/`relationshipType`; the same from/to/type created by two parties are two distinct Relationship elements — relation identity and assertion identity are deliberately fused (https://spdx.github.io/spdx-spec/v3.0.1/model/Core/Classes/Relationship/).
- **For.** in-toto Statement v1: "Subject artifacts are matched purely by digest, regardless of content type"; the statement binds subject digests to a `predicateType`/`predicate`; who attests lives in the signing envelope outside the statement, so one statement can carry many signatures (https://github.com/in-toto/attestation/blob/main/spec/v1/statement.md). DSSE's PAE, `PAE(type, body) = "DSSEv1" + SP + LEN(type) + SP + type + SP + LEN(body) + SP + body`, is the envelope's framing (https://github.com/secure-systems-lab/dsse/blob/master/protocol.md).

## Consequences for G0-B.1

- **Ruling (1) agrees with the prior art** (RDF 1.2 triple term/reifier, Wikidata statement/reference, PROV-O qualification, in-toto statement/envelope) and **departs only from SPDX 3 and property graphs**, which mint a fresh relationship id per assertion. Keep the "explicit semantic qualifier" in the relation key minimal — RDF 1.2 treats "different characteristics" as reifier-level, so anything that varies per source belongs on the ASSERTION, not in the relation key (https://www.w3.org/TR/rdf12-concepts/).
- **Ruling (2) agrees with RDFC-1.0 and Hogan**: an anonymous node's identity is a hash of the quads that mention it, and the container is that mention — so yes, the container belongs in the hash. One gap: RDFC-1.0 escalates to N-degree hashing when first-degree hashes collide; hash(container ‖ sentence) has no escalation, so two byte-identical sentences in one round's list collide silently. Either add the list ordinal to the pre-image or emit a DUPLICATE_FINDING fault at projection time (https://www.w3.org/TR/rdf-canon/).
- **Separator: length-prefix or a domain tag is the common practice, bare NUL is not.** RDFC-1.0 and trusty URIs hash newline-terminated sorted lines; Git hashes `type SP size NUL content` (https://git-scm.com/book/en/v2/Git-Internals-Git-Objects); DSSE PAE length-prefixes every field; RFC 6962 prepends 0x00/0x01 because "This domain separation is required to give second preimage resistance" (https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6962#section-2.1). NUL is safe only because `container_lid` is a constrained lid that cannot contain NUL; add a fixed domain tag (e.g. `g0-finding\0`) so a finding hash can never collide with any other G0 hash domain that shares the alphabet.
- **Ruling (3) departs from all prior art in resolving a bare token at all**; CURIE requires a binding, JSON-LD leaves the string unexpanded, identifiers.org/Bioregistry require a prefix and only canonicalise its form. The non-blocking UNQUALIFIED_REFERENCE fault is the right compensation and "ambiguous → no edge" matches CURIE's MUST. Because uniqueness depends on the *pinned ledger set*, record the ledger-set hash on the resulting ASSERTION so a later pin cannot silently flip a resolved reference to ambiguous on replay (https://www.w3.org/TR/curie/).
- **Ruling (4) agrees with Tantow et al. 2025 and with replay-as-verification**; Soufflé and Nemo confirm "rule id + antecedent facts per derived fact" as the standard shape, and Doyle's SL-justification (consequent, IN-list, OUT-list) maps directly onto stratified negation. Worth adopting: emit the DAG (shared-subproof) form and mirror the CertifyingDatalog JSON encoding, which buys a second, formally verified checker for free (https://github.com/knowsys/CertifyingDatalog).
- **Nanopublications are not a counter-example to (1)** even though their id hashes assertion + provenance together: the trusty URI identifies the *publication* (an ASSERTION-level object), while the assertion graph's content is the proposition-level payload — the same two-level split G0 is adopting (https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1809.06532).
