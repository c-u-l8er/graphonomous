# R1 — Provenance models for Graphonomous G0

Research lane R1 for the G0 architecture decision (deterministic, content-addressed, provenance-preserving projection over git-versioned registries; ~200–2,000 entities, ~500–10,000 relations). All sources were read on **2026-09-02**; the list with access dates is at the end. Each section closes with a verdict: **reference pattern** (understand it) or **adopt as concept** (build the idea, not the stack).

## Executive summary

- **Separate the proposition from its occurrences** (RDF 1.2, CR 7 Apr 2026: a *triple term* is the abstract proposition, a *reifier* one occurrence of it, many-to-many). G0: one content id per statement, a separate record id per *assertion of it* (source + evidence state).
- **RDF 1.2 chose transparency; the 2021 CG report chose opacity.** For "what did this file literally say", opacity is the right instinct: keep the source's literal bytes on the observation record and resolve identity in a separate, recorded derived step.
- **PROV supplies vocabulary, not storage.** Entity/Activity/Agent, qualified `wasDerivedFrom` (activity + *plan*), `wasRevisionOf`, `wasInvalidatedBy`, `specializationOf` and bundles map one-to-one onto source records, adapters, derivations, supersession and adjudication. Use the names as field names; emit PROV-O only on export.
- **The nanopublication envelope is the right record shape**: assertion / provenance / publication-info, immutable under a content hash covering the whole record, superseded by a *forward pointer in the new record*, retracted by a *separate signed record*. That is WRL's discipline already.
- **Property-graph edge identity is what reification was always buying.** The W3C tutorial equates a Turtle 1.2 reifier with a Cypher edge id. G0 edges should be first-class records with ids and properties; do not adopt GQL/Cypher.
- **Store the derivation DAG, read it as a semiring.** Rule id + premise ids per derived fact is the minimal representation a UI can unfold into a proof tree (Soufflé keeps only rule number + proof height; Nemo caches traces into a DAG); alternatives are `+`, joint premises `·`, and commutativity makes the digest traversal-order independent.
- **Git is already the transaction-time log** — no second event log, no bitemporal store. Keep Datomic's one idea: a retraction is a *positive record* (op = false) with its own provenance, so the current view is a pure function of the snapshot.
- **Absence is not falsity.** Negative and unknown states are explicit records; closed-world conclusions are sound only when scoped to a finite registry at a named commit (semipositive Datalog: negation over EDB only); rules with negation are stratified over observed facts and the rebuild fails on a negative cycle.
- **Type your unknowns.** FHIR's `data-absent-reason` is the pattern: `unknown` is a value with a reason code, distinct from `not-applicable` and `masked`.
- **Canonicalization is solved, with known traps.** RFC 8785 (JCS) matches WRL's sorted-keys/compact form and documents the number/Unicode caveats; RDFC-1.0 shows why minted anonymous ids are the enemy of a deterministic digest.

## 1. RDF 1.2 / RDF-star: triple terms and reifiers

**Status.** Concepts and Semantics are Candidate Recommendation snapshots of 7 April 2026 ([W3C news](https://www.w3.org/news/2026/w3c-invites-implementations-of-rdf-1-2-concepts-and-abstract-data-model-and-rdf-1-2-semantics/)); Turtle, N-Triples and Schema are 2026 Working Drafts, the Primer a Group Note Draft (dates in Sources).

**Triple terms.** "An RDF triple used as the object of another triple is called a triple term" — object position *only* ([Concepts](https://www.w3.org/TR/rdf12-concepts/); grammar `tripleTerm ::= '<<(' subject predicate object ')>>'`, [N-Triples](https://www.w3.org/TR/rdf12-n-triples/)). A triple term denotes a proposition: `I(E) = IT(I(E.s), I(E.p), I(E.o))`, class `rdfs:Proposition` ([Semantics](https://www.w3.org/TR/rdf12-semantics/); [Schema](https://www.w3.org/TR/rdf12-schema/)). "In a given RDF graph, a triple can appear as a triple term, an asserted triple, or both."

**Reifiers.** "A reifying triple is a triple where the predicate is `rdf:reifies` and the object is a triple term. The subject of that triple is called a reifier." A reifier "may denote a variety of things … such as a statement or belief that the proposition holds", and the relation is many-to-many: "There can be multiple, distinct reifiers related to the same abstract proposition, such as statements with different sources … One reifier may also be used to reify multiple, distinct propositions" ([editor's draft](https://w3c.github.io/rdf-concepts/spec/)). The Turtle forms ([Turtle 1.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/rdf12-turtle/)):

```
<<( :s :p :o )>>                        # triple term — nothing asserted
<< :s :p :o ~ :r >> :accordingTo :x .    # :r rdf:reifies <<( :s :p :o )>> — base NOT asserted
:s :p :o {| :statedBy :bob |} .         # asserts :s :p :o AND mints a reifier
:alice :name "Alice" ~ :stmt1 ~ :stmt2 . # two reifiers, one proposition
```

**Difference from the 2021 CG report.** The [CG Final Report (17 Dec 2021)](https://w3c-cg.github.io/rdf-star/cg-spec/2021-12-17.html) allowed quoted triples as subject *or* object, made them unique ("wherever `<< … >>` appears, it always denotes one and the same thing") and referentially opaque ("Two quoted triples containing distinct but equivalent terms … are nonetheless considered to be different triples"). RDF 1.2 inverted both: "RDF terms that appear in a triple term have the same denotation as when they appear in an asserted triple … we say that triple terms are transparent", and the occurrence node was split out so one proposition can have many annotated occurrences. The WG's ISWC 2025 tutorial: "RDF 1.2's triple terms are almost identical to … RDF-star's quoted triples … RDF 1.2 only allow triple terms in the object position", but "Turtle 1.2 is significantly different from Turtle*" — `<< :s :p :o >>` is a *triple term* in Turtle-star and a *reifier* in Turtle 1.2; `{| |}` puts properties "on the triple term itself" versus "on a reifier" ([slides](https://www.w3.org/Talks/2025/iswc-tutorial-rdfsparql-12/slides/2-foundations.pdf)). The deck also nests reification for provenance-of-provenance: `_:b rdf:reifies <<( _:a rdf:reifies <<( :s :p :o )>> )>>`.

**Lesson.** Statement identity (hash of canonical s,p,o) and assertion identity (hash of statement + source + state) are different objects; two sources asserting one statement, or one statement in two evidence states, are two assertion records over one statement id, never two statements. A claim without commitment is representable: `<< >>` "references it without implying its truth" ([Primer](https://www.w3.org/TR/rdf12-primer/)).

**Pitfalls.** (a) Asserted vs unasserted is *syntactic* — `<< >>` does not assert, `{| |}` does — so mixing them silently loses claims. (b) Transparency permits substitution inside quoted content: the tutorial derives `<< cs:rburton cs:married cs:liz ~ _:wlr_2 >>` from `owl:sameAs`; for "what the file said" that is wrong, and the CG's opacity was motivated by recording "the precise language used" ([w3c/rdf-star#22](https://github.com/w3c/rdf-star/issues/22)). (c) Reifier denotation is under-specified: "There is no definition of what it means to 'correspond'…" ([rdf-star-wg#169](https://github.com/w3c/rdf-star-wg/issues/169)). (d) The [Interoperability note](https://www.w3.org/TR/rdf12-interop/) covers only Full↔Basic; there is no CG migration path. (e) Old-style `rdf:Statement` is "not semantically related (one does not entail the other)" to the new mechanism and is non-normative in RDF 1.2 Schema.

**Verdict: adopt as concept** (proposition/occurrence split; transparency by choice); **reference pattern** for the syntax.

## 2. W3C PROV

[PROV-DM](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-dm/), [PROV-O](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-o/) and [PROV-N](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-n/) are Recommendations of 30 April 2013. An agent "bears some form of responsibility for an activity taking place, for the existence of an entity, or for another agent's activity"; `wasDerivedFrom` is "a transformation of an entity into another, an update of an entity resulting in a new one, or the construction of a new entity based on a pre-existing entity" (subtypes Revision, Quotation, PrimarySource); `wasInvalidatedBy` is "the start of the destruction, cessation, or expiry of an existing entity". A specialization "shares all aspects of the latter, and additionally presents more specific aspects of the same thing". A bundle is "a named set of provenance descriptions, and is itself an entity, so allowing provenance of provenance to be expressed." The qualified form is one term: `wasDerivedFrom(ex:d; e2, e1, a, g2, u1)` — derived, source, activity, generation, usage. [PROV-Links](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-links/) (Note) adds `prov:mentionOf`, kept out of the Recommendation because "The concept Mention is experimental".

| G0 thing | PROV |
|---|---|
| source record (file/key/commit) | `Entity`; the commit is one of its fixed aspects |
| adapter run | `Activity` `wasAssociatedWith` a `SoftwareAgent`; **`hadPlan` = the TRVM program's content-bound id** |
| derived fact | `Entity` `wasGeneratedBy` the run; qualified `wasDerivedFrom` each premise |
| supersession | new `wasRevisionOf` old; old `wasInvalidatedBy` the run; both `specializationOf` the stable claim |
| adjudication (GPT / Fable / human) | `Activity` `wasAssociatedWith` `Person`/`SoftwareAgent`; delegation via `actedOnBehalfOf` |
| receipt / rebuild | `Entity` `wasGeneratedBy` the adjudication, `wasAttributedTo` the agent; a rebuild snapshot is a `Bundle` |

**Over-engineering at this scale:** the qualified-influence pattern on every edge (three extra nodes per derivation), PROV-Constraints, `prov:mentionOf`, Collections, instants on everything. Carry PROV-named fields (`generatedBy`, `derivedFrom[]`, `plan`, `attributedTo`, `invalidatedBy`) inside the G0 record and generate PROV-O when a consumer wants it.

**Verdict: adopt as concept** (three roles, qualified derivation with a plan, revision/invalidation, bundles); **leave** the ontology as storage.

## 3. Named graphs, datasets, nanopublications

A dataset is one default graph plus named graphs, each "a pair consisting of an IRI or a blank node (the graph name), and an RDF graph"; "the graph name is not required to denote the graph … RDF does not place any formal restrictions on … the relationship between that resource and the graph" ([RDF 1.1 Concepts](https://www.w3.org/TR/rdf11-concepts/)). The WG "did not standardize the semantics of RDF datasets" and catalogued seven incompatible readings, including quoting ("Alice said: 'Bob is smart'" without asserting it) ([Datasets note](https://www.w3.org/TR/rdf11-datasets/)). N-Quads carries the graph label as a fourth term ([N-Quads](https://www.w3.org/TR/n-quads/)). So the fourth element is a *container*, not an assertion context; whether a quad is asserted is a policy you must state.

Nanopublications state it. A nanopub is head + assertion + provenance + publication-info graphs with mandatory `np:hasAssertion / hasProvenance / hasPublicationInfo` links; provenance is "how this came to be … who generated it, when … where was the assertion obtained from" and must reference the assertion; pubinfo must reference the nanopub URI ([guidelines](https://nanopub.net/guidelines/working_draft/)). Identity is a *trusty URI* — SHA-256 over normalised quads, computed last, "which therefore also covers the signature" ([Kuhn et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7959648/)) — so "resources keep their hash values even when presented in a different format" ([Trusty URIs](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.5775)). "A nanopublication is persistent, you can never edit nor delete it"; updates carry `npx:supersedes` in the *new* nanopub's pubinfo; retraction is a separate nanopub asserting `<agent> npx:retracts <np>`, valid only if "signed with the same public key as the nanopublication that it retracts" ([docs](https://nanopub.readthedocs.io/en/latest/publishing/retraction.html)).

**Is the trio a good shape?** Yes, as a *record envelope*: it forces the separation G0 needs, is hash-first, and solves supersession without deletion. Do not take it literally as four RDF graphs per statement — group coherent statements per record and keep statement ids distinct from record ids.

**Verdict: adopt as concept** (envelope, forward-pointer supersession, retraction-as-record, hash covers everything); **leave** RDF datasets as the model.

## 4. Property graphs: GQL, openCypher, PG-Schema/PG-Keys

ISO/IEC 39075:2024 was "officially published 2024-04-11"; "Property graph databases store and retrieve nodes (vertexes) and edges (relationships)" and data "can either be schema-free or constrained by a full Graph Type" ([JTC1 overview](https://jtc1info.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/2024-Article-39075-Database-Language-GQL.docx.pdf)). openCypher: "a directed, vertex-labeled, edge-labeled multigraph with self-edges, where edges have their own identity"; a relationship has "exactly one relationship type", "a unique, comparable identity" and "a set of properties" ([openCypher](https://github.com/opencypher/openCypher/blob/master/docs/property-graph-model.adoc)).

**What edge properties buy over reification.** Identity and attributes on the edge are native, so `source`, `commit`, `receipt`, `evidence_state` sit on the relation with no extra node, and two sources asserting the same relation are two parallel edges. The W3C tutorial makes the equivalence explicit: `(Santiago)-[LA380:flight {company:"LATAM"}]->(Arica)` is `:Santiago :flight :Arica ~ :LA380 {| :company "LATAM" |}` — the reifier *is* the edge id. Costs: a property is a value, so provenance-of-provenance needs the edge promoted to a node; and a multigraph legalises duplicates, so deterministic rebuild needs declared identity keys — [PG-Keys](https://ldbcouncil.org/docs/papers/pg-keys.pdf) (SIGMOD '21): keys "applicable to nodes, edges, and properties", in modes "exclusive, mandatory, and singleton". [PG-Schema](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.10962) (PACMMOD 2023) adds typed nodes and edges (edge types carry properties) and `STRICT` conformance.

**Standards for property-graph provenance:** none. GQL has no provenance vocabulary; the research pattern is semiring annotation — "edges of a graph are annotated, in addition to usual properties, with elements of a semiring" ([Ramusat, Maniu, Senellart, EDBT 2021](https://openproceedings.org/2021/conf/edbt/p16.pdf)) — plus a 2025 ADBIS paper on bitemporal property graphs ([DOI 10.1007/978-3-032-05281-0_15](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-032-05281-0_15), pointer only; paywalled).

**Verdict: adopt as concept** (edges are records with ids, properties and declared identifier keys); **leave** GQL/Cypher/DBMS.

## 5. Datalog provenance: semirings, why/how/where, proof trees

[Green, Karvounarakis, Tannen (PODS 2007)](https://web.cs.ucdavis.edu/~green/papers/pods07.pdf) annotate tuples with elements of a commutative semiring — the relational identities hold "if and only if (K,+,·,0,1) is a commutative semiring" — and show `N[X]` is most general: for any valuation there is "a unique homomorphism of semirings Eval_v : N[X] → K". A polynomial reads as proof structure: `2s² + rs` means "(f,e) is computed by q in three different ways; two of them use the input tuple s twice; the third uses input tuples r and s." Recursion sums "over all its derivation trees … the product of the tags of the leaves" (formal power series), and "subtraction is not allowed" — negation is outside. [Cheney, Chiticariu, Tan (2009)](https://homepages.inf.ed.ac.uk/jcheney/publications/provdbsurvey.pdf): lineage = all contributing tuples; why-provenance = the *witness basis*, whose minimal form is invariant under equivalent queries; how-provenance = the polynomial, which "hints at the structure of the proofs" but is rewriting-sensitive; where-provenance = the source *locations* a value was copied from.

**Engines.** Soufflé stores per tuple only the rule number and "the minimal height of the proof tree", rebuilding proofs lazily by backward search, including `explainnegation` ([docs](https://souffle-lang.github.io/provenance)), at "1.27x on average" overhead ([Zhao, Subotić, Scholz, TOPLAS 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.05045)). Nemo produces "a proof tree that shows the concrete rule applications and input facts … tracing is very efficient and adds no cost to reasoning" ([KR 2024](https://proceedings.kr.org/2024/70/kr2024-0070-ivliev-et-al.pdf)); it walks backwards from the fact's table through the rule to the body facts, and "We avoid recomputing traces for the same fact by caching each result, which internally creates a directed acyclic graph (DAG) representing the proof" ([EvonNemo 2024](https://imld.de/cnt/uploads/2024-XLoKR-EvonNemo.pdf)). [PUG](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.05752) covers negation: why-not is why over the negated query. [Deutch, Milo, Roy, Tannen (ICDT 2014)](https://openproceedings.org/ICDT/2014/paper_36.pdf): Boolean-formula provenance "incurs a super-polynomial size blowup" even for linear Datalog; PTIME poly-size *circuits* avoid it.

**Minimal derivation representation for a UI proof tree.** One record per rule application:

```
derivation   := { rule: <content-bound program id>#<rule index>,
                  premises: [fact_id…] (body order), bindings?: {...} }
derived_fact := { statement_id, derived_by: [derivation_id…] (sorted) }
```

The tree unfolds on demand along `premises`; shared sub-proofs make it a DAG (Nemo's cache, Deutch's circuits). Canonical ordering (premises by body position, alternatives by id) makes the term canonical JSON → one hash, so a derived edge carries a compact derivation identity. Semirings are then evaluations of the same DAG — Boolean (derivable?), counting, why (witness sets), min-trust (weakest premise). Store the DAG, not polynomials.

**Verdict: adopt as concept** (derivation DAG with rule ids; semiring as a read); Soufflé's two-integer annotation is the fallback if space matters.

## 6. Supersession and time

Datomic: a datom is "an immutable atomic fact that represents the addition or retraction of a relation between an entity, an attribute, a value, and a transaction" — `[e a v tx op]`; "change is represented by accumulating the new, not by modifying or removing the old"; transactions are entities with `:db/txInstant` and arbitrary attributes ([data model](https://docs.datomic.com/whatis/data-model.html)); `history` "includes the present and the unfiltered past, including retractions" ([filters](https://docs.datomic.com/time/filters.html)); excision exists but "should never be used to correct erroneous data" ([excision](https://docs.datomic.com/reference/excision.html)). Bitemporal: "valid time, the time period during which a row is regarded as correctly reflecting reality by the user of the database" versus "transaction time, the time period during which a row is committed to or recorded in the database"; SQL:2011 gives application-time period tables and system-versioned tables with start/end column pairs ([Kulkarni & Michels, 2012](https://sigmodrecord.org/publications/sigmodRecord/1209/pdfs/07.industry.kulkarni.pdf)); XTDB makes system time immutable and valid time "a user-managed dimension" ([XTDB](https://docs.xtdb.com/concepts/key-concepts.html)). Event sourcing: "Capture all changes to an application state as a sequence of events"; state is a rebuildable projection and corrections are reversing events ([Fowler](https://martinfowler.com/eaaDev/EventSourcing.html)).

**Smallest model for G0.** G0 is rebuilt from a git snapshot, so *git already is the transaction-time log* (commit = Datomic's tx entity). Minimal design: (1) every record carries `{repo, path, key/line, commit}`; (2) supersession is a forward edge in the new record (nanopub `npx:supersedes`, PROV `wasRevisionOf`), retraction its own record (Datomic op = false, `npx:retracts`), never a mutation; (3) the current view is a pure function — current iff no record in the snapshot supersedes or retracts it — so "as-of" collapses to "which snapshot", and history is the union across snapshots or the git log; (4) valid time only where a source states it. No bitemporal store, no second event log, no in-place `status` field.

**Verdict: adopt as concept** (retraction as positive record; commit as transaction entity); **reference pattern** for bitemporality.

## 7. Negative and unknown knowledge

OWL: "If some fact is not present in a database, it is usually considered false (the so-called closed-world assumption) whereas in the case of an OWL 2 document it may simply be missing (but possibly true), following the open-world assumption"; explicit negatives need `NegativeObjectPropertyAssertion` ([OWL 2 Primer](https://www.w3.org/TR/owl2-primer/)). SHACL validates the data graph as given ("full RDFS inferencing is not required"): `sh:minCount` absence is a violation, `sh:closed` forbids unlisted properties, and the report is `(focusNode, resultPath, sourceShape, sourceConstraintComponent, severity)` ([SHACL 2017](https://www.w3.org/TR/shacl/); [SHACL 1.2 Core WD](https://www.w3.org/TR/shacl12-core/)). Knublauch: "OWL has been designed for classification tasks (inferencing in an 'open world'), while SHACL covers data validation (in a 'closed world')" (an OWL `maxCardinality 1` breach is inferred sameness; SHACL reports it) ([SHACL and OWL](https://spinrdf.org/shacl-and-owl.html)).

Negation-as-failure: "`not B` succeeds when all attempts to prove B fail in finite time", justified by Clark completion, which "can be inconsistent" (`p ← not p`) ([Sergot](https://www.doc.ic.ac.uk/~mjs/teaching/KnowledgeRep491/NBF_491-2x1.pdf)). Abiteboul–Hull–Vianu: `{p ← ¬q, q ← ¬p}` "has two minimal fixpoints … {p} and {q}" and "no criteria can exist that would always permit identification of a unique intended model"; semipositive programs negate only EDB relations under "the closed world assumption"; a stratification puts a negated IDB predicate in a strictly lower stratum, a program is stratifiable "iff its precedence graph has no cycle containing a negative edge", the semantics is independent of the stratification chosen, and well-founded semantics is the 3-valued fallback ([Chapter 15](http://webdam.di.ens.fr/Alice/pdfs/Chapter-15.pdf)); Nemo, Soufflé, RDFox and VLog all ship stratified negation (KR 2024, Table 1). Explicit unknowns: FHIR's `data-absent-reason` says "why the normally expected content of the data element is missing" — `unknown` (with children such as `asked-unknown`, `temp-unknown`, `not-asked`), `masked`, `not-applicable`, `error`, `not-performed`, `not-permitted` ([FHIR R5](https://hl7.org/fhir/valueset-data-absent-reason.html)).

**Rules for G0.** (1) An absent edge means "no record", never "false". (2) Negatives are explicit, *scoped* records: `NOT_FOUND{registry, commit, adapter}` is a sound closed-world claim because the registry is finite and enumerated. (3) OPEN/UNKNOWN are evidence states with reason codes. (4) Rules with negation are stratified over observed facts; the rebuild refuses a negative cycle. (5) "Why is X not a primitive?" is answered by a *positive* derivation of `not_primitive(X)` from an adjudication or NOT_FOUND record (PUG's why-not-as-why), never by failure to find. (6) Checks over the projection use a SHACL-shaped report; claims stay open-world.

**Verdict: adopt as concept** (scoped negatives, typed unknowns, stratification, SHACL-shaped reports); **leave** OWL reasoning and unstratified semantics.

## What to adopt as concept vs what to leave

**Adopt (concept only, on WRL/TRVM):** (1) `statement_id = sha256(canonical s,p,o)` and `assertion_id = sha256(statement_id + source anchor + state)`, for nodes and edges alike; (2) literal source bytes on the observation record, identity resolution as a recorded derivation; (3) PROV names as field names, `plan` = TRVM program id, rebuild = `Bundle`; (4) nanopub envelope, forward-pointer supersession, retraction-as-record, hash over the whole record; (5) edges as records with ids, properties and declared identifier keys; (6) derivation DAG `{rule, premises[], bindings?}`, canonically ordered and content-addressed, semirings evaluated on demand; (7) retraction as a positive record, commit as transaction entity, current view as a pure function of the snapshot; (8) scoped negatives, typed unknowns, stratified negation over observed facts, SHACL-shaped check reports; (9) JCS-equivalent bytes with [RFC 8785](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8785)'s caveats (IEEE-double numbers only, big integers as strings, no Unicode normalisation) and [RDFC-1.0](https://www.w3.org/TR/rdf-canon/)'s lesson that anonymous ids defeat a digest.

**Leave:** RDF datasets as the storage model; full PROV-O qualification per edge, PROV-Constraints, `prov:mentionOf`, Collections; the CG's opaque quoted triples and old-style `rdf:Statement`; GQL/Cypher/DBMS and PG-Schema DDL (borrow only STRICT as a validation mode); stored polynomials, formal power series, probabilistic semirings; bitemporal tables, Datomic itself, any event log separate from git; OWL for constraint checking; unstratified negation and well-founded semantics; the nanopub server network and RSA signing unless records cross a trust boundary.
